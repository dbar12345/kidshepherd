Kid Shepherd: Privacy Policy

Last updated: 8 November 2026

Kid Shepherd helps parents guide how their children use their iPhone or iPad. It's built so that we, the developer, can't see your family's data.

Who we are

Kid Shepherd is developed by Dmitriy Barash ("we"). Contact: dbar12345@gmail.com.

The short version

  • We run no servers and keep no accounts. Kid Shepherd stores data on your devices and in Apple iCloud (CloudKit).
  • Everything one family device sends to another is end-to-end encrypted on the device. Neither Apple nor we can read it.
  • No ads, no tracking, no analytics, no selling of data. We don't share data with third parties.
  • The child can always see what parents can see and do (What my parents can see & do).

Never collected: messages, chats, photos, contacts, browsing content, keystrokes or screen contents. Screen sharing for live help is Apple's own FaceTime feature, used only when the child starts it and shares their screen.

How data moves

Devices pair with a one-time code and create encryption keys that never leave the devices. Messages are encrypted and signed, then stored briefly in Kid Shepherd's iCloud container so the other device can fetch them. Every stored message also has a few unencrypted fields:

  • random device identifiers;
  • a timestamp;
  • a short generic notification text, such as "⏰ New time request", with no names or details.

Senders delete their messages after 7 days. Commands expire after 24 hours.

Purchases

Kid Shepherd Plus is an optional subscription sold through Apple's In-App Purchase. Apple handles the payment; we never see your card or bank details. The app only learns whether the family has Plus and when the current period ends, and keeps that on the parent's device. No purchase information is sent to the child's device or to us.

Notifications

Apple's push service wakes the app and shows the generic text above. Details are shown only after the app decrypts the message on the device.

Children

Kid Shepherd is designed to be set up by a parent or guardian. We don't knowingly collect personal information from children for ourselves: everything stays within the family's devices and iCloud, encrypted, as described above. The app has no ads or third-party analytics.

Your choices

  • Turn location sharing or place alerts off at any time (parent), or deny location permission in iOS Settings (device).
  • Remove a device or a parent in the app: the devices stop exchanging messages.
  • Deleting Kid Shepherd removes its data from that device. Messages left in iCloud expire as described above.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update the date above and describe the change in the app's release notes.
